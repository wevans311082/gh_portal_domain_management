# Shared registrant validation workstreams

Updated 28 October 2026 following the requirement to group checks and assign TLD lists, rather than build an independent KYC process for every extension.

## Workstreams

| Workstream | Shared responsibility | Reviewed membership / exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| Contact accuracy and entity type | Actual individual or organisation, reachable email, address, country and telephone; provider field bounds. Individuals do not need a company or Companies House record. | All enabled profiles. Provider contact types and field bounds remain profile constraints. |
| Identity and organisation assurance | Reusable, dated evidence against the current contact digest, reviewer and policy; separate person and business checks. | Customer/contact scope, independent of TLD. No automatic identity approval from declarations or contact formatting. Reuse only where evidence scope, freshness and policy permit it. |
| Registry eligibility | Shared declaration adapters and explicit registry exceptions. | General: com, net, org, io. UK accuracy: uk, co.uk, org.uk, me.uk; me.uk requires an individual. US nexus: us. EU presence: eu prepared, ordering still gated. |
| Registrar verification | Provider evidence, association checks, bounded refresh/resend and expiry. | ICANN RAA profile: com, net, org. Registry-specific profile: io, uk, co.uk, org.uk, me.uk, us. Registry-specific means the RAA workflow is not applicable; it does not mean verified. |
| Publication and privacy | Explicit consent, permitted privacy, withdrawal and reconciliation. | Existing organisation publication consent: com, net, org. Privacy capability remains explicit in each profile; UK and US profiles prohibit the optional service. EU prepared profile also prohibits it. |
| Domain operations | Shared paid submission, transfer, renewal, contact change and recovery workflows; profile-specific limits and holds. | All enabled profiles; UK routing, US registrant-only nexus and EU prepared delegation constraints remain explicit exceptions. |

## Implementation and rollout

`apps/domains/registrar_policy.py` is the reviewed membership catalogue. `PROFILE_TLDS` maps each shared profile to an explicit list. Catalogue construction rejects duplicate assignments, so a new list cannot silently replace another profile. Profiles select eligibility and email workstreams and service-contact routing. Shared contact serialization dispatches through the selected eligibility workstream.

Contact type, provider name/company/postcode limits and the documented empty-company representation now come from that same profile. The legacy model helper no longer guesses contact types for unreviewed extensions. Prepared serialization is explicitly separate from ordering permission, and an enabled reviewed profile takes precedence over its earlier prepared contract.

Publication consent and contact-change authorization have independent profile memberships. An organisation-publication policy change must never disable the IRTP checks required before applying a contact replacement. New registration submission snapshots record the selected workstreams; earlier accepted paid attempts remain retained and are not resubmitted by this metadata change.

Incoming transfer admission now has its own explicit profile membership as well. Four contact roles do not implicitly enable a transfer. The shared authorization-code workflow is assigned to com, net, org, io and us; UK-family and prepared EU transfers still need their distinct workflows. Checkout, paid preparation, direct API calls and owner reconciliation use the same capability gate. Provider/profile code bounds and normalized nameserver limits are checked before the relevant mutation. New transfer preparation captures the selected policy and code constraints without saving the code in its audit snapshot; policy changes during preparation stop the paid submission. An already acknowledged transfer retains its earlier result and is never purchased again because a profile changed.

The [transfer endpoint](https://manage.resellerclub.com/kb/answer/758), checked 29 October, permits some codes to be supplied during registrar approval. This includes the current reviewed US workflow; a supplied US code still needs 6-16 characters without whitespace/control characters. Profiles can separately require a code at submission. Cart entry validates common storage bounds, preserves valid supplied characters rather than trimming the secret, and rechecks the selected profile before invoicing. Customer/staff inputs are masked with linked guidance. Existing plaintext authorization-code storage and the broader transfer progress/cancel workflow remain tracked under roadmap item 7; masking is not encryption.

The shared captured-contact renewal check applies to US nexus and the prepared EU-presence profile. `.me.uk` uses the same check through a small explicit personal-namespace exception, while sharing the UK contact profile. It requires exactly one matching registration/transfer source, accepted submission evidence, the current provider scope, actual role IDs and an unchanged captured contact digest, or a matching confirmed contact replacement. It rereads domain/customer state and respects open contact-change holds. No identity/business approval is invented or required by this check. Unreviewed extensions, including prepared EU ordering, require review before creating a renewal invoice. Standard enabled profiles without an additional declaration continue through their existing renewal path.

The assurance workstream uses one owner-only local contact review for identity and business scopes. Business scope supports overseas organisations and sole traders without a UK company number, while individuals have no business-review requirement. Local review never writes registry-eligibility or registrar-email outcomes. Direct registrar-email reconciliation, as well as UI actions and scheduled scans, is restricted to the reviewed email-workstream list before any provider read.

Customer 360 now exposes the shared mapping in a native expandable section per domain. Staff can see the requirement group, assigned extension list and any namespace exception alongside the separate verification results. It does not claim checks have passed merely because a policy applies. Existing domain permissions filter the section, including after role changes; billing-only staff receive no domain disclosures.

Provider-contract follow-up, checked 29 October: the [ResellerClub eligibility endpoint documentation](https://manage.resellerclub.com/kb/kb/answer/1505) describes a contact-ID/status map but still does not enumerate its status values. No success interpretation was invented. The user confirmed that no sandbox is configured. Those contract checks remain pending; local grouping and interface verification do not depend on production access.

The [agreement foundation](registrar-agreements.md) now assigns immutable approved text versions to these same shared profile lists, rather than making independent document systems for each TLD. Customer receipts bind the exact domain, operation, contact version and document set. Owner publication and customer cart/direct-request review and receipt attachment are implemented. New paid registration/transfer admission now enforces current acceptance and retains the admitted version manifest. New cart invoices, quote conversion and direct registration billing now enforce current acceptance before allocation. Direct registration uses a draft/review/invoice flow that preserves its existing billing treatment. Manual bundled hosting/invoice choices now survive agreement review through the retained follow-up workflow, with original prices, current permissions and queue/worker fences. These shared flows do not create per-TLD KYC implementations.

Prepared contracts are separate from enabled membership. Saving or testing EU declarations does not enable EU ordering. Unknown extensions still require review; sharing a suffix category, contact type or apparent geography is insufficient to enable one.

Complete and verify each workstream across its assigned lists before expanding membership. New TLD onboarding should normally add a reviewed mapping and exception tests, rather than duplicate identity forms, evidence storage, queues or paid submission logic. Add a new adapter only when a documented registry requirement cannot be represented by an existing one.

Acceptance checks: individuals and organisations remain supported; local checks never create external verification evidence; evidence is not reused across incompatible scopes; service contacts do not inherit registrant-only declarations; unknown/prepared extensions remain gated; existing operation and privacy restrictions survive regrouping. ResellerClub payload and response contracts must be reviewed before enabling additional membership. This refactor does not claim new registry approval or live provider verification.

Verification on 29 October: broad suite 1,974 passed with the existing deployment assertion excluded; final email membership changes passed 127 focused tests. Six further delegation tests verify external-server acceptance and rejection of child hosts, hidden servers, inconsistent counts and foreign customer associations. No live registrar requests were made.

The final suite, including the additional authorization-independence regression, passed 2,031 tests with the unchanged deployment assertion excluded. Fourteen registry desktop/mobile states passed accessibility checks; the renewal-review mobile screen was visually inspected. This verifies application behaviour with mocked providers, not external registry certification or production concurrency.
